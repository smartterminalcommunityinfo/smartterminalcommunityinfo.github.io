Key Players

The individuals involved in the development, permitting, and facilitation of the TriTexas Logistics Park in San Marcos, Texas.

All information on this page is drawn from public records, city permitting documents, and government filings obtained through Public Information Requests. Source documents are available upon request.

Developer

Scarborough Lane Development is the firm of record for the TriTexas Logistics Park project. It is controlled by El Paso billionaire Paul L. Foster through Franklin Mountain San Marcos I, LP. The individuals below represent Scarborough Lane in city communications, permitting applications, and pre-development meetings with City of San Marcos staff.

James Feagin
Scarborough Lane Development / Landmark Interests
Founder-emeritus, Scarborough Lane; Founder & President, Landmark Interests

Founder of Scarborough Lane Development (the developer of record) and founder/president of its brokerage arm, Landmark Interests. He is not an officer of Franklin Mountain - the Foster entity that owns the land - but is listed as tax/property contact on Caldwell CAD records for all FM-I LP parcels, and his name appears in city communications and permitting records spanning the full permitting period. See Ownership Structure for the full corporate chain.

Ryan Burkhardt
Scarborough Lane Development / Landmark Interests
Principal

Burkhardt attended the May 2022 pre-development meeting with City of San Marcos staff as a developer representative and is regularly copied on permitting correspondence. He holds roles at both Scarborough Lane Development and a related entity, Landmark Interests, and is named as a "Prepared By" party on project documents.

Clayton Kendall
Scarborough Lane Development
Representative

The most frequently appearing developer representative in city records, with his name appearing in 167 documents. Kendall attended the 2022 pre-development meeting alongside Burkhardt and is regularly included on permitting and engineering correspondence between the developer team and City of San Marcos staff.

Scot Whitwer
Scarborough Lane Development
Representative

Whitwer is named in permitting coordination emails between the developer and city engineering staff, including correspondence about construction inspections and conditional permit releases for the Phase I public improvement construction plan.

Lead Applicant Engineer

Kimley-Horn, a national civil engineering firm, served as the primary engineering consultant for the developer throughout the permitting process.

Ben Ruby
Kimley-Horn
Lead Engineer, P.E. - San Antonio Office

Ruby served as the lead licensed engineer on all major permitting submissions for the project, including the Public Improvement Construction Plan (PICP), Watershed Protection Plan (WPP), Conditional Letter of Map Revision (CLOMR), and plat applications. His name and email appear more than 860 times across PIR documents - more than any other individual - reflecting his central role in the day-to-day engineering and permitting work.

San Marcos City Council

The Mayor and six Council Members are elected at-large to staggered terms and hold final authority over annexation ordinances, development agreement amendments, and zoning changes tied to the project. Below is every identified piece of City Council legislation touching the project (2019–2025) with each then-serving member's vote where it is individually recorded. No finalized City Council meeting minutes have been published since roughly mid-2022 - for every item after that point, the aggregate outcome is known but individual roll-call votes are not published anywhere in the public record found to date. That gap is marked explicitly rather than guessed at. 2026 is an election year: Mayor Hughson is not seeking re-election as Mayor and is instead running against incumbent Alyssa Garza for Place 3; Amanda Rodriguez has announced a run for Mayor.

2026 Mayoral Candidates

Three candidates are on the ballot for Mayor in the Nov. 3, 2026 election: Amanda Rodriguez (current Place 6 Council Member, profiled above), Christopher "Crispy" Polanco, and Ryan Thomason. Thomason's prior council tenure and business history are documented below.

Ryan Thomason
City of San Marcos - Former Place 5 · 2026 Mayoral Candidate
Former Council Member
Elected in a Dec. 2009 runoff · served Jan. 2010–Nov. 2015 · Mayor Pro Tem 2011–2012 · did not seek re-election · legal name Michael Ryan Thomason

Co-owns Wood and Thomason Construction, LP with Christopher Wood, who simultaneously served as a Planning & Zoning Commissioner. Both were named as owners of the firm in permit records for Concho Commons, a Casey Development student apartment project seeking two additional floors on an already-approved 13-story downtown building. Thomason told Council he had "unanswered questions about a potential conflict of interest" and sought to recuse himself from the vote "pending further exploration." His business partner Wood did not recuse himself from a similar P&Z vote on the same project and voted in favor of the additional floors (Dec. 9 vote).

After leaving Council, petitioned the City directly as a private applicant for his own development: a plat variance (PVC-15-02, Feb. 9, 2016, P&Z) and Final Plat (PC-15-49_03, Mar. 8, 2016, P&Z) for Lodges at Parker's Pond, LLC - also associated with an Out-of-City Utility Extension request by the related entity "Parkers Pond, Ltd." for ~44.70 acres (ID#16-20, Jan. 19, 2016, City Council). Also associated with Lindsey Homes, LP; no Legistar record found under that name. (His wife works for Deloitte & Touche, LLP, which separately holds unrelated City audit/CDBG-DR consulting contracts - not a Thomason business connection.)

Filed for Mayor in 2026 against Amanda Rodriguez and Christopher "Crispy" Polanco. Outgoing Mayor Jane Hughson is serving as his campaign treasurer.

City of San Marcos

Although the project site sits primarily in Caldwell County, the City of San Marcos has jurisdiction over the property through its Extraterritorial Jurisdiction (ETJ), utility service agreements, annexation proceedings, and a 2023 Development Agreement. The following city staff members reviewed, approved, and coordinated on those matters.

Joe Pantalion
City of San Marcos - City Manager's Office
Assistant City Manager, P.E.

Pantalion attended the May 2022 I-Zone pre-development meeting and served as a primary city leadership contact for the project. He coordinated meetings between city staff and the developer to discuss wastewater solutions - a documented constraint on the development - and responded to public concerns about the AN-24-03 road annexation.

Richard Reynosa
City of San Marcos - Engineering
Assistant Director of Engineering, P.E./CFM

Reynosa was the primary city engineering contact for all permitting on the project, reviewing and responding to PICP, WPP, and CLOMR submissions. He appears in more than 28 PIR documents and coordinated the city's response to drainage, roadway, and utility questions raised by the developer's engineering team.

Amanda Hernandez
City of San Marcos - Planning
Planner, AICP/CNU-A/CPM

Hernandez was the lead planning contact for the project, attending the 2022 pre-development meeting and handling planning review for annexations, zoning, and the development agreement. She served as a point of contact between the developer, engineering staff, and the City Manager's Office on planning matters related to the project.

Tyler Hjorth
City of San Marcos - Utilities
Former Director of Utilities (retired late April 2026)

Hjorth attended the 2022 pre-development meeting as the city's utilities authority and was a key contact for discussions about water, wastewater, and electric service to the development. He was later included in correspondence about a proposed regional sewer infrastructure plan along Highway 80 East, which would affect the project's ability to obtain wastewater service. Succeeded by Paul Kite on electric utility matters near the airport following his retirement.

Greg Schwarz
City of San Marcos - Engineering
Senior Engineer

Schwarz served as the City's primary technical reviewer for the project's watershed protection plan (WPP) submissions. He presented the TriTexas Logistics Park QWPP Phase 2 (Permit #2024-49696) to the P&Z Commission on August 27, 2024, and issued the R1 comment letter for the Edwards Aquifer parcel (Permit #2025-55457) flagging impervious cover violations, missing TCEQ permits, and peak flow increases for all regulated storm events. He also addressed floodplain and drainage regulations at the January 8, 2019 P&Z reconsideration hearing.

Julia Cleary
City of San Marcos - Planning
Senior Planner

Cleary served as the lead planning contact for the 2023 re-application phase, presenting ZC-23-01 to the P&Z Commission on March 28, 2023 with a staff recommendation of approval. She was also involved in annexation coordination for AN-24-03 (the Energy Parkway ROW annexation). In 2026, she confirmed to colleagues that only ~700 of the ~1,980–2,000 marketed acres are currently annexed and zoned into the City - a gap between the site's marketed scale and its actual jurisdictional footprint.

Helen Ramirez
City of San Marcos - Economic & Local Business Development
Director

Ramirez emerged in spring 2026 as the primary city contact fielding nearly every new prospective-tenant RFI for the site (Apex Graphite, Roller Coaster, Fiber Optics, Neighborhood Club, Skate Board, Haute Couture, Ellie, Apex Prime), coordinating directly with Hays-Caldwell EDP. She announced the rejection of Project Apex Graphite (Allied Graphite) on May 8, 2026, citing Development Agreement covenant restrictions, and forwarded the May 4, 2026 Austin Business Journal article announcing the SMART Terminal/Axis → TriTexas Logistics Park rebrand to city staff.

Rodney Gonzales
City of San Marcos - City Manager's Office
Assistant City Manager

In April 2026 internal correspondence about the Project Apex Graphite (Allied Graphite) prospect, Gonzales invoked an unwritten policy that city staff should not assist site searches for "projects and business types that are intentionally excluded in our Economic Development Policy," to avoid sending "a false signal of supporting these types of projects" - discussion that preceded the site's official rejection of that project on covenant grounds.

Economic Development

The Hays-Caldwell Economic Development Partnership (EDP) is a public-private organization that markets sites in Hays and Caldwell counties to prospective industrial tenants. Internal city records show EDP staff routed confidential Requests for Information (RFIs) for large industrial projects directly to city engineering staff, specifically proposing TriTexas Logistics Park as a candidate site.

Emily Mathes
Hays-Caldwell Economic Development Partnership
Vice President of Economic Development

Mathes sent city engineering staff a confidential RFI for "Project Vortex Wind" - a proposed battery cell and pack assembly facility with a capital investment of up to $2.3 billion and 2,000 jobs - explicitly recommending TriTexas Logistics Park as the preferred site and asking city staff to respond to utility capacity questions on a tight deadline. The RFI originated from the Office of the Governor.

Sarah Martin
Hays-Caldwell Economic Development Partnership
Project Manager

Martin forwarded a separate RFI for "Project Frontier" - an architectural products manufacturer seeking 60-80 acres with a $178 million capital investment - to city engineering staff and asked them to confirm water and wastewater capacity for the TriTexas Logistics Park site. The RFI was distributed through Opportunity Austin, a regional economic development organization.

State of Texas

The Office of the Governor's Economic Development and Tourism division actively recruits large industrial projects to Texas and connects them with local sites and incentives. Records show the office was involved in directing at least one major prospective tenant toward the TriTexas Logistics Park site.

Lauren Slusher
Office of the Governor - Economic Development & Tourism
Industry Recruitment Specialist

Slusher is an industry recruitment specialist with the Governor's Economic Development and Tourism office, which received the initial inquiry for "Project Vortex Wind" from a consulting firm representing a prospective battery manufacturing tenant. The office then passed the RFI to the Hays-Caldwell EDP for regional response, with Axis Logistics Park identified as the candidate site.

Caldwell County

The project site sits in Caldwell County. The following county official appeared on the public record in connection with the project.

Ed Theriot
Caldwell County
County Commissioner, Precinct 3

Theriot appeared at the January 8, 2019 P&Z Commission meeting and made himself available to answer questions about the county's role in the project. He referenced a county 381 Agreement, EDA Grants, and roadway projects planned by TxDOT and the Capital Area Metropolitan Planning Organization (CAMPO) related to Loop 110, US-80, and State Highway 21 - signaling county-level coordination and support for the SMART Terminal project at its inception. The project site falls within Precinct 3.

Dual role: in his day job, Theriot (AICP-certified) is a Planner at Drenner Group, an Austin land-use consulting and real estate law firm, specializing in entitlement services for mid-rise and high-rise mixed-use developments and "government liaison work" for developers. His own firm's bio confirms both roles side by side. He was a consultant on Concho Commons (now Aspire San Marcos, 101 Concho St.) - the same Casey Development student housing project at the center of the Ryan Thomason/Christopher Wood conflict-of-interest episode (see 2026 Mayoral Candidates section) - commenting publicly on unit availability and the project's expected draw of new downtown consumers.

As Drenner Group's agent, Theriot has personally petitioned San Marcos P&Z on behalf of numerous private development clients, appearing as the named applicant representative on at least 10 land-use cases from 2020–2022: ZC-20-03 (Sportsplex, for Outlet West Investors, Ltd.), PC-20-05 (Genlyte Industrial Park Replat, for Joseph Joyce), ZC-20-10 (The Barracks, for McCoy Family Partnership One), ZC-20-24 (2400 Block of Hwy 123 / Picoma, for Jaime J. Russek), CUP-21-03 (Windemere Event Venue, for Eden Farms, Ltd.), CUP-22-01 (Fredericksburg Purpose Built Student Housing), CUP-22-02 and CUP-22-26 (Just A Closet Storage), ZC-22-08 (Hwy 123 Business Park), and ZC-22-34 (221 E. McCarty Ln / Sienna Pointe). Separately, on March 4, 2025, San Marcos City Council discussed and confirmed Theriot as Caldwell County's designated representative to the Economic Development San Marcos (EDSM) Board (ID#25-178) - a distinct body from the Hays-Caldwell EDP that fields TriTexas/Axis tenant RFIs, but still a City economic development board discussing confidential prospective-tenant matters in executive session (see "Project Bushwood" in `businesses_summary.html`).

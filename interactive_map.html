Interactive Map

The TriTexas Logistics Park site (formerly SMART Terminal / Axis Logistics Park) and its surroundings, drawn from public records. Turn layers on and off in the layer list, click the map to see what is at that spot, or look up an address.

Background

Layers

Tick a group name to turn on all of its layers. Hover a shape or a layer name to highlight it. Click the map to list every switched-on layer at that spot, with its source. Developer-drawn layers are approximate. Copy link saves the current view and layers as a link you can share.

How the layers were made

  • Parcels (TriTexas ownership, EDC Austin, Project Irish) are the Caldwell Central Appraisal District's parcel outlines. Appraisal maps are close but are not surveys.
  • Caldwell 142 Reinvestment Zone #2 is traced from the 23 recorded boundary calls in Exhibit A to Caldwell County Order 01-2027 (Kimley-Horn survey dated October 27, 2026). The traced boundary closes within 0.02 feet and measures 611.01 acres, and it was placed on the map by fitting it to the appraisal district's outline of the 660.503-acre tract (average difference about 5 feet).
  • Developer land use (industrial, commercial, greenbelt, the Reedville buffer and the container storage area) is traced from Kimley-Horn's Exhibit B: Conceptual Land Use Plan (issued April 5, 2023). The exhibit was placed on the map by matching its project boundary to the appraisal district's parcel lines (median difference about 12 feet), so these areas are approximate, as drawn by the developer.
  • Development phases are traced by color from Exhibit C: Conceptual Phasing Plan and placed with the same fit as Exhibit B; traced acreages are within about 6% of the labeled 825, 343 and 808 acres.
  • Earthship Project Irish shows the 19 tracts listed in the October 23, 2025 development agreements (15 in Irish North, 4 in Irish South). Sixteen are whole appraisal district parcels matched to the agreements' surveys by acreage, location and deed. Three are parts of larger parcels: a 239-acre parcel was divided along the surveyed line between two listed tracts, and the 358.23-acre Irish South tract was drawn from its 18 surveyed boundary calls and fit to parcel lines (median difference about 3 feet). The tracts were under purchase contract, not owned, when the agreements were approved. Owner names are not shown for the four small tracts owned by private individuals.
  • Homes are appraisal district parcels whose improvements carry a residential state code (single-family, mobile home, multifamily or rural home) or that have a homestead exemption, from the 2026 certified Caldwell CAD export. Homesteads are owner-occupied homes. Tracts over 25 acres are left off so whole farms are not shaded. No names or addresses are shown.
  • Martindale city limits and ETJ are from the Caldwell Central Appraisal District's public GIS. Streams are from the USGS National Hydrography Dataset.
  • Former San Marcos Municipal Landfill is traced from Figure 5 of the U.S. Department of Labor's Gary Job Corps Center Draft Environmental Assessment (July 2022). It was placed by fitting that figure's Tract IV and V outlines to the appraisal district parcels those tracts became (median difference about 14 feet). Fuel dumps and military landfill spots are from the sketch map in the EPA's 1995 Site Inspection Prioritization report for the Gary Job Corps Center, which is labeled "not to scale" and records a 1987 recollection; the sketch was placed by matching its runways to the airport (within about 150 feet), so these spots are approximate. The 0.5- and 0.8-mile rings mark the distances at which that report found contaminated residential wells southeast of the landfill; the wells themselves are not marked.
  • Detention ponds and drainage channels are traced from Kimley-Horn's Axis Detention and Water Quality Summary Exhibit, which is drawn on the same sheet as Exhibit C and placed with the same fit. Its road matches the built Energy Parkway within about 20 feet. Approximate, as drawn by the developer.
  • Allowed cut and fill depth (Exhibit G) was placed by aligning that exhibit's aerial background with Exhibit B. Approximate, as drawn by the developer.
  • Municipal utility districts are the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality's official water district boundaries.
  • Runway 35 approach surface is the FAA Part 77 approach surface for the airport's ultimate Runway 35, from the City's "Ultimate Scenario Part 77 Surfaces" exhibit. The exhibit was placed by matching its parcel lines to appraisal district parcels (median difference about 8 feet), and the surface was redrawn from its standard dimensions (1,000 to 4,000 feet wide over 10,000 feet, rising 1 foot in 34). Heights above ground use USGS ground elevations and are approximate.
  • Water and sewer service areas are Certificate of Convenience and Necessity (CCN) boundaries from the Public Utility Commission of Texas.
  • San Marcos city limits and ETJ are from the City of San Marcos GIS. Floodplain is FEMA's National Flood Hazard Layer (1% and 0.2% annual chance zones). Pipelines are from the Railroad Commission of Texas public pipeline map; the RRC notes its pipeline locations are approximate. Rail is from OpenStreetMap.
  • Possible area of the 319.589-acre removal request is this site's own estimate and is unconfirmed. The City's record for case PDA-22-07(B) says only "Removing approximately 319.589 acres of land from the development agreement" and gives no location. The shape shown is the part of Reinvestment Zone #2 on Franklin Mountain's 660.503-acre tract, chosen because its area on the appraisal map (about 318 acres) is close to the acreage in the application. It may be wrong.
  • Proposed annexation (AN-26-07) is the outline the City of San Marcos draws for that case on its planning map (Proposed Development layer), copied as of November 14, 2026. It is a pending request.
  • LCRA Timmerman Power Plant shows the appraisal district parcels near Maxwell owned by the Lower Colorado River Authority and LCRA Transmission Services Corporation (about 51.5 acres together). Plant details are from LCRA's website.
  • Address search sends the text you type to Esri's address lookup service and marks the result on the map. This site does not store it, and it is not added to shared links. The distance shown is a straight line to the nearest edge of the Franklin Mountain land as drawn by the appraisal district.

Map colors

  • Developer land use uses the American Planning Association's standard land-use colors (industrial purple, commercial red, open space green, residential yellow, public facilities blue). Flood zones use FEMA's flood map colors. Water is blue, sewer green and gas yellow, following the utility industry's uniform color code. City limits are solid lines and ETJs dashed; incentive zones are pink, and other projects are white outlines.

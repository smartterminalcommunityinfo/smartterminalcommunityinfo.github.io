Project North Star

Active - Decision July/August 2026 Advanced Magnetics Manufacturing (U.S. company)
RFI Date: March 2026
Source: Texas Governor's Office EDT
Site Need: Not specified (10,000 MT annual capacity)
Investment: $1.8 billion
Jobs: 700 (197 within 2–3 years)
NAICS: 3346 (Magnetic & Optical Media Mfg.)
Decision Date: July / August 2026
Production Start: Q2 2029

Operations

U.S. magnetics company producing "world's first high-performance rare-earth-free permanent magnets." 10,000 metric tons annual capacity. Strategically important for defense and EV supply chain.

Water / Wastewater

1,400,000 GPD (1.4 MGD) water. 800,000 GPD (0.8 MGD) wastewater. City notes water is "high but manageable" and encourages water reuse. Wastewater deemed acceptable.

Critical Infrastructure Note

City of San Marcos Response

City finds water demand "high but manageable" and encourages water reuse strategies. Wastewater is acceptable. SH 80 Lift Station CIP completion updated to Summer 2028, which supports the Q2 2029 production start timeline. Site visit expected April/May 2026.

  • Most strategically significant project in pipeline: rare-earth-free magnets are critical for EV motors, wind turbines, and defense applications - currently dominated by Chinese supply chain.
  • Q2 2029 production start provides more realistic infrastructure runway than most other projects.
  • $1.8B investment and 700 jobs - second largest by investment in the active pipeline.
  • Site visit expected April/May 2026; final decision July/August 2026.
Source: Governor's Office RFI / North Star emails, March 2026

Project Apex Graphite (Apex Anode Plant)

Rejected May 8, 2026 Battery Materials - Graphite Anode Refining
RFI Date: April 2026 (site tour April 7)
Company: Allied Graphite (formerly Urbix)
Site Need: 50 ac Phase 1 → 100–130 ac total
Investment: $207.4M Phase 1 / up to $584M total
Jobs: 156 → up to 448 ($80,338 avg.)

Operations

Refines raw graphite into coated spherical purified graphite (CSPG), an anode input material for lithium-ion batteries - not battery cell manufacturing itself. Partnered with Brazilian graphite producer Graphcoa. Ran through site-selection contractor JLL. Previously scouted the site in 2024 as "Project Chestnut" but was tied to a $100M+ federal grant contingent on an Alabama location; reworked the grant to allow a site change.

Water / Power

12" water line sufficient. Power: 5 MW currently available; 54 MW ultimate load requires a new transformer and two dedicated lines - est. $7,000,000, 3-year timeline, via Bluebonnet Electric Coop at $0.071/kWh. Wastewater contingent on the new SH-80 Lift Station (conservative Q1 2028 / best case Q4 2027).

Building Height Conflict

Company's stated need was a 60–70 ft max building/stack height - exceeding the site's own 62 ft ordinance cap (Development Code §4.4.5.4) even before the covenant issue arose.

City of San Marcos Response

Helen Ramirez (Director of Economic & Local Business Development) told HCEDP on May 8, 2026: "Given the covenant restrictions on the property we do not see this project being viable at Axis site." This matches the Development Agreement's Exhibit D prohibited-use list, which expressly bars "Manufacturing of carbon batteries" - the rejection rationale is consistent with the actual governing document.

  • Internal city discussion preceded the "official" rejection: Asst. Director of Engineering Richard Reynosa wrote April 24 that the project "is not going to pass City Council authorization for any kind of Economic Incentive," and Assistant City Manager Rodney Gonzales cited an unwritten policy against assisting "projects and business types that are intentionally excluded in our Economic Development Policy" to avoid "a false signal of supporting these types of projects."
  • Site-selection documents (JLL RFI log, 54 items) independently flagged San Marcos Regional Airport proximity as a potential "fatal flaw," and pegged overall site buildability at "~83%."
  • Senior Planner Julia Cleary noted only ~700 of the ~1,980–2,000 marketed acres are currently annexed/zoned into the City - a gap between marketed scale and actual jurisdiction.
Source: Allied Graphite Business Deck, JLL RFI/Site Assessment logs, city email correspondence, April–May 2026

Key Intermediaries
Hays-Caldwell Economic Development Partnership: Sarah Martin and Emily Mathes served as local contacts coordinating with the Governor's Office on all RFIs.
Texas Governor's Office EDT Staff: Philip Rocha, Lauren Slusher, Jennifer Kolbe, Stephanie Mazurkiewicz-Muehlstein, Joe Magruder, and Sandra Sanchez distributed and managed RFIs.

Background (2016–2017)
December 11, 2014
Legal Cotton Center Consent Agreement & MUD Petition Approved (MUD-14-02 / Ord. 2014-70)
City Council approves the original Cotton Center Consent Agreement and processes the Municipal Utility District (MUD) petition for the Cotton Center area in Caldwell County. The Consent Agreement governs water/wastewater service obligations and is tied to the City's Sewer Certificate of Convenience and Necessity (CCN No. 20116). This agreement — signed by Walton Texas LP and related entities — becomes a foundational document that persists through 2025.
  • MUD-14-02: Petition for MUD by Metcalfe, Wolfe, Stuart & Williams (on behalf of landowners)
  • Ord. 2014-70: Ordinance approving the Consent Agreement and MUD proceedings
  • Caldwell County recording: Document No. 2014-006144
Source: San Marcos Legistar — MUD-14-02, Ord. 2014-70; MUD-14-02 - FINAL Consent Agreement 10 24 14.pdf
December 20, 2016
Legal Cotton Center Development Agreement Executed
An earlier development agreement covering parcels along Highway 80 in Caldwell County is executed between the City of San Marcos and original landowners. This becomes a predecessor document to the SMART Terminal agreement.

P&Z P&Z Commission Denies ZC-18-21 — 934 Acres to Heavy Industrial (Vote: 5–4)
The Planning & Zoning Commission votes to deny zoning case ZC-18-21 — Franklin Mountain / Ohnheiser's request to rezone approximately 934 acres from ETJ to Heavy Industrial for the SMART Terminal. The denial triggers a Council reconsideration motion.

October 17, 2019
Legal City Approves Chapter 380 Incentive Agreement with Katerra Construction (Res. 2019-225R)
City Council approves Resolution 2019-225R — a Chapter 380 Economic Development Incentive Agreement specifically with Katerra Construction, LLC for the Whisper PDD facility near IH-35 and Yarrington Road. 2020–2021
January 21, 2020
City Council Martindale ETJ Boundary Agreement (Res. 2020-17R) — Postponed 7–0 After Executive Session
City Council considers Resolution 2020-17R — an agreement with the City of Martindale defining extra-territorial jurisdiction (ETJ) boundaries adjacent to the SMART Terminal site. The resolution is tabled before executive session, then postponed 7–0 following the executive session (§551.071 — legal advice on Martindale ETJ matters). The agreement is never adopted.
  • Motion to table until after exec session: Derrick/Mihalkanin — 7–0
  • Motion to remove from table: Mihalkanin/Rockeymoore — 7–0
  • Motion to postpone: Mihalkanin/Derrick — 7–0
  • Mayor Hughson's post-exec announcement: "Item #15 Martindale ETJ is postponed."
  • Pre-meeting citizen comment by Mary Beth Harper: stated Martindale was meeting the same night to finalize their part of the agreement; noted the ETJ dispute over the SMART Terminal site
The unresolved Martindale ETJ boundary dispute remains an open issue through at least 2023 and underlies Martindale's April 2023 formal resolution opposing the SMART Terminal.
Source: San Marcos City Council Regular Meeting Minutes, January 21, 2020 (CivicEngage: /Minutes/_01212020-2007); San Marcos Legistar — Res. 2020-17R, Res. 2020-17R(b)
2021
Legal Development Agreement Assigned to Franklin Mountain San Marcos I, LP
Following Katerra's bankruptcy, the development agreement is assigned from Tack Redwood Partners (who acquired the ~660-acre tract from Walton Texas / RUC Holdings ULC, an Alberta, Canada corporation) to Franklin Mountain San Marcos I, LP.
  • Assignee assumes "all rights, duties and obligations" of the agreement
  • Creates a legal tension: the 2019 agreement said obligations would not bind future owners, but this assignment explicitly transfers them
  • Recorded in Caldwell County

Legal Franklin Mountain Completes Land Assembly — Three Additional Acquisitions in 2022
Beyond the October – December 2021 Texas Transportation Alliance / Walton parcels, Franklin Mountain San Marcos I, LP acquires three additional properties to complete its ~2,017-acre portfolio:
  • Mar 31, 2022 (2022-002523): 0.95 acres at 2862 FM 1984 (Homesite parcel, A021) from Van Lee LLC (Ohnheiser-linked entity from original 2019 development agreement). Property had ~$197K in improvements at time of acquisition; structure later removed by 2024.
  • Jun 16–17, 2022 (2022-004831/4832): 130.296 acres on Quail Run Rd, Martindale (A021) from Tack Redwood Partners, who had acquired it from James A. Neumann the same day — a same-day pass-through conveyance.
  • Jul 7, 2022 (2022-005483): 143.444 acres at 10920 Hwy 142, Maxwell (A188 Maxwell, Thomas) from Best David Matthew. This parcel is in the Lockhart ISD / San Marcos ISD boundary area and carries an agricultural exemption.
With these acquisitions, FM-I LP's assemblage spans approximately 1,983 acres across 8 CAD parcels, plus 75 acres held by FM-75 LP — total ~2,058 acres.

Milestone City Council Adopts New Development Agreement — ~2,017 Acres (Res. 2023-020)
City Council approves the First Amended and Restated Development Agreement with Franklin Mountain San Marcos 1, LP for approximately 2,017 acres (Case No. PDA-22-07). This supersedes the 2019 agreement and formally defines the SMART Terminal project at its full scale.

April 20, 2023
Opposition City of Martindale Formally Opposes SMART Terminal
The Martindale City Council adopts Resolution 2023-04-20 formally opposing heavy industrial zoning of the San Marcos Air, Rail, and Truck Terminal (SMART Terminal). Primary concern: flooding. The resolution cites regional flood disaster history and argues that heavy industrial development on the site would worsen flooding impacts on Martindale and surrounding communities.

April 21 – May 10, 2023
City Council Council Initiates DA Review; Mayor Hughson Questions Environmental Standards; Multiple Executive Sessions (ID#23-314, 317, 327, 340, 363)
Intense period of public action and closed-session legal discussions on the SMART Terminal Development Agreement:
  • May 2 (ID#23-314, public meeting): Council receives staff presentation and votes to initiate a formal review of the SMART Terminal DA with potential amendments. Mayor Jane Hughson poses substantive environmental questions on the public record:
    • What is the applicable TSS (Total Suspended Solids) removal standard? Staff: 89% in the Edwards Aquifer Recharge Zone, 85% in Transition/Contributing Zones, 80% in the River Protection Zone
    • Does SMDC §6.2.3.5 (impervious cover mitigation exceptions) apply given the DA's 70%/90% grants? Staff: section applies, but developer already agreed to higher standards under DA §§2.01–2.02
    • Can the review be accelerated before the 90-day window closes July 3? Staff: Council remains within the window; first reading can be moved up if discussions conclude sooner
  • Apr 24 (ID#23-317): Executive session — Legal advice regarding SMART Terminal Development Agreement
  • Apr 27 (ID#23-327): Executive session — Real property discussions and legal advice
  • May 4 (ID#23-340): Executive session — Legal advice regarding SMART Terminal Development Agreement
  • May 10 (ID#23-363): Executive session — Legal advice regarding SMART Terminal Development Agreement
No DA amendment appeared on the July 3, 2023 regular agenda; the outcome of the 90-day review is not reflected in any subsequent public action.

Impact Fees & the 2019 Rate Freeze

How San Marcos calculates wastewater impact fees, why the City just proposed to roughly quadruple them, and why the SMART Terminal / TriTexas Logistics Park development agreement locks the developer into paying 2019-era prices instead.

Important disclaimer: This analysis is built from official public records — a City of San Marcos ordinance (Ch. 86.294), the City's March 2026 Wastewater Impact Fee Final Report, and the developer's development agreement summary. One figure in the City's own cost report did not internally reconcile (flagged below in the caveats section) — the dollar comparisons here should be read as directionally solid, not audit-grade.
The bottom line
≈ $17.9 million
The estimated gap between what SMART's development agreement locks it into paying in wastewater impact fees, and what the City's own 2026 study says that same amount of sewer capacity actually costs to provide today.

Impact fees

An impact fee is a one-time charge the City assesses against new development to recover the cost of the water/sewer capital infrastructure that development's demand requires — pipes, lift stations, treatment capacity — rather than spreading that cost across the existing ratepayer base.

Calculation method (Sec. 86.294)

San Marcos Code of Ordinances Sec. 86.294 sets the fee as a two-factor calculation:

  1. LUEs (Living Unit Equivalents). A connection's expected peak flow (gpm) is converted to LUEs — one LUE is roughly the flow demand of one single-family connection; large industrial users scale to dozens or hundreds of LUEs. The conversion methodology itself is defined in Sec. 86.297, which was not in the records reviewed for this page.
  2. Fee-per-LUE ("Schedule 1"). The City publishes a per-LUE dollar rate that it revises periodically as it re-studies capital infrastructure costs.
Fee = LUEs × rate-per-LUE (schedule in effect). With LUE count fixed by contract, the variable that determines a project's total bill is which rate schedule applies — which is where the SMART Terminal agreement diverges from current policy.

Rate schedule history

Sec. 86.294 lists five base (1.0 LUE) rates for water and wastewater service, each reflecting a periodic re-study of infrastructure cost:

March 19, 2019 — the date the SMART Terminal agreement's fee freeze refers to — falls between the 4/30/2018 update and the proposed 9/30/2026 update. No rate change happened in between, so "2019 rates" and "the after-4/30/2018 schedule" are the same number.

Development agreement provisions

The 2023 SMART Terminal development agreement (superseding the original 2019 agreement) sets two terms that determine the fee outcome:

  • Impact fees frozen at March 19, 2019 rates — fixed regardless of subsequent City fee schedule updates.
  • 2,108 LUEs of wastewater service guaranteed — contractually reserved sewer capacity.

Driver of the 2026 rate increase

The March 2026 Wastewater Impact Fee study lists the specific capital projects the updated rate is designed to fund. One of them serves this development directly:

SMART Development (FM 110) Gravity Main — a new 18-inch sewer line along FM 110 that also decommissions the aging Gary Job Corps lift station. Total estimated cost: $24,078,600. The City's own utilization study attributes 63% of this project's need to growth between 2025–2035, growth the study ties explicitly to the proposed SMART development.

The 2026 rate update to $11,161/LUE is calculated to recover costs like this project's. SMART's fee, frozen at $2,684/LUE, recovers a fraction of what the study attributes to its own growth-driven infrastructure need.

Funding the gap

Impact fees are one of the primary funding sources for growth-driven infrastructure such as the SMART Development Gravity Main. A shortfall between the fee collected and the study's cost basis doesn't eliminate the need to build the infrastructure. Under standard municipal utility financing, such a gap is typically covered by some combination of:

  • Existing utility ratepayers, through water/sewer bills
  • City debt (bonds), repaid over years by the utility system as a whole
  • General utility revenue that could otherwise fund other system needs or rate relief

This page does not have documentation showing which of these the City intends to use — that would require the City's specific CIP funding/financing plan, which wasn't in the records reviewed here.

Caveats

  • LUE math not verified. The ordinance section that defines how a specific development's peak flow gets converted into an LUE count (Sec. 86.297) was not available in the records reviewed. The 2,108 LUE figure comes from the development agreement summary, not from an independent recalculation.
  • The City's own cost report has an internal inconsistency. The itemized cost sheet for the SMART Development Gravity Main project (in the March 2026 report) totals $9,631,500 on its own line-item math, while the report's summary table lists the same project at $24,078,600 in three separate places. The same mismatch appears on an adjacent project's detail sheet, suggesting a row-reference/formatting error in how the source PDF was assembled — not something resolvable from the document alone. This page uses the $24,078,600 summary-table figure, which is corroborated in multiple places.
  • The proposed 9/30/2026 rate schedule is a proposal, not yet a final adopted rate as of this document's "Proposed Revisions Shown" title — confirm current status before citing it as an adopted fee.

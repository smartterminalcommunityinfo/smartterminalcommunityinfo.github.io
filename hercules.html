Project Hercules: The Daimler Agreements

Daimler Truck North America plans a truck plant on about 611 acres in Caldwell County, between SH 80 and SH 142. The San Marcos City Council votes on the agreements for it on December 1 and December 15, 2026. This page explains each item in plain terms, quoting the City's documents.

The three December 1 items

Item 10: Res. 2026-215R, removing 319.589 acres from the SMART Terminal agreement

Franklin Mountain San Marcos 1, LP and the City would sign a "Modification to Amended and Restated Development Agreement." It states: "The Removal Tract is hereby released from the Development Agreement and the Removal Tract shall no longer be governed or regulated by the Development Agreement."3 The same resolution gives "initial authorization" for a new development agreement for the combined 611 acres.4 One vote; it includes a public hearing.

  • The release only takes effect on "the last of the following events to occur": Daimler buys the land and gives the City proof; the City enters the Chapter 380 agreement; an interlocal agreement with Caldwell County "establishing standards for development of the Removal Tract" is approved; and the land is released from "Caldwell County's Irish development agreement."3
  • "if the Effective Date does not occur by June 1, 2027, this Agreement shall be void and of no force or effect."3
  • "All other terms and conditions of the Development Agreement shall remain in force and effect," including "the owner's responsibility to complete all off-site public improvements."3

Item 11: Res. 2026-219R, interlocal agreement with Caldwell County

An agreement between the City and the County on who reviews and approves development on the 611 acres. "The Parties agree that the City shall serve as the primary entity responsible for reviewing and approving development applications, plans, and improvements," acting "as the County's contractor" for County functions. On the County land, "City review of building plans shall be limited to verifying compliance with the owner's building-code covenants in the Hercules County Development Agreement, and City fire-safety review."5 One vote. The agreement itself is not attached to the item (see documents not yet posted).

Item 12: Res. 2026-220R, Chapter 380 and 212 agreement with Daimler (first of two readings)

An 82-page agreement between the City and Daimler that is two agreements in one: a Chapter 380 economic development agreement (tax rebates for jobs and investment) and a Chapter 212 development agreement (rules for the land before and after annexation).1 Franklin Mountain signs a "Joinder and Consent" but "assumes no obligation under the Agreement."1 The second reading is set for December 15.6 Its main terms are summarized below.

What the Daimler agreement says

All quotes are from the Chapter 380 and 212 agreement attached to Res. 2026-220R for the first reading.1

Jobs and wages

  • "at least 800 Jobs no later than December 31, 2030," 1,600 by the end of 2031, 2,400 by the end of 2032, and "at least 3,200 Jobs no later than December 31, 2033." From 2034, "not fewer than 3,200 Jobs." (Section 3.04)
  • "The minimum average annual wage for all Jobs ... is $64,989.00," adjusted for inflation every five years starting in 2034. "No minimum wage applies to any individual Job." (Exhibit A, Section 3.08)
  • Daimler will "make good faith and commercially reasonable efforts to recruit in the City." "Local recruitment is not a condition to any Grant Payment." (Section 3.04)

Investment

  • Cumulative investment of at least $233 million by the end of 2029, $466 million by the end of 2030 and $699 million by the end of 2031, plus the land price counted once. (Section 3.01)
  • Daimler "shall begin operation of the Business on the Project Site on or before December 31, 2030." (Section 3.03)

Tax rebates

  • Up to 20 yearly "Grant Payments" equal to a share of the City's maintenance and operations property tax on the site: 75% for years 1-5, 70% for years 6-10, 60% for years 11-15 and 55% for years 16-20. (Section 2.24)
  • Year 1 is the first tax year after the site is annexed. "No Property Taxes are levied, and no Grant Payments accrue, for any period before Year 1." (Section 2.24)
  • City staff estimate the rebates at "approximately $60.68 Million dollars," with the City keeping "$60.69 Million."2

If targets are missed

  • "In no event shall the Company's failure to satisfy any Jobs Milestone, Capital Investment milestone, or the Maintenance Threshold constitute a default or non-compliance ... or a basis for termination of this Agreement, and the City's sole remedy for any such failure shall be the reduction or withholding of the Grant Payment for the Year in which the failure occurs." (Section 10.01)
  • The same applies to the average wage: "the City's sole remedy for a shortfall shall be the reduction of the Grant Payment for that Year." (Section 3.08(e))
  • If North American Class 6-8 truck production or sales drop 20% or more, deadlines are extended, up to 36 months in total. (Section 10.05)

Annexation

  • Daimler delivers an annexation petition "within thirty (30) days after the later of (i) Construction Completion and (ii)" the City's zoning steps. The City "shall not annex the Project Site or any part of it" before then. (Section 6.03)
  • "Construction Completion shall occur no later than December 31, 2030; if Construction Completion has not occurred by that date, as extended, Construction Completion shall be deemed to have occurred on that date." (Section 2.13)
  • The land outside the ETJ is added to the ETJ when Daimler buys it, at Daimler's request. (Section 6.03(f))
  • After annexation the site is zoned Heavy Industrial (HI). (Section 6.05)
  • "If the Company fails or refuses to petition for annexation ... for reasons not caused by the City, then the City Development Agreement Release shall lapse as to the Project Site," and the SMART Terminal agreement terms come back. (Section 6.13)

What can be built

  • "No Data Center, and no facility specifically providing power to a Data Center, will be allowed or constructed on the Project Site." (Section 3.02) Computing for Daimler's own operations is allowed if it does "not exceed fifty percent (50%) of the combined floor area of the manufacturing facilities." (Section 2.17)
  • Permitted uses include the truck plant, with "paint, coating, and finishing operations," "vehicle test tracks," "the bulk storage and dispensing of fuels," "Distribution Center," "Terminal, truck, freight or rail," and "Every other use that is permitted by right in the HI District." (Exhibit J, Part I)
  • The 26 prohibited uses from the SMART Terminal agreement carry over, with exceptions for the plant, for example storage of "fuels, batteries, paints, coatings, solvents, lubricants, compressed and industrial gases" used in the business. Data centers and sexually oriented businesses are added. (Exhibit J, Part II; compare the SMART Terminal list)

Water and wastewater

  • Water comes from Maxwell Special Utility District; "the City's obligations under this Agreement do not include water service." (Section 6.10) City staff: "approximately 300,000 gallons of water per day at full capacity."2
  • The City reserves "not less than three hundred (300) living unit equivalents" of wastewater capacity, "approximately 300,000 gallons per day." (Section 5.02)
  • Wastewater rate: $4.64 per 1,000 gallons until the "Rate Hold Date," then $5.11, then $5.13, then a 3.5% yearly increase, never above the City's lowest non-residential rate. The City's current rates are "$9.34 per 1,000 gallons inside the City and $11.67 per 1,000 gallons outside the City." (Exhibit C, item 6)
  • "Wastewater impact fees for the Project shall be assessed at the rates in effect on March 19, 2019." (Section 6.10) See Impact Fees & the 2019 Freeze.
  • "Before connecting the Project Site to the City wastewater system, the Company shall plug ... any water well on the Project Site that is not retained for a lawful use approved by the City." (Section 6.10)

Environmental permits and air

  • "The City shall not oppose, and shall not request a contested-case hearing on, any application by the Company for a permit from the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality or the United States Environmental Protection Agency that is consistent with the Project." (Section 5.03)
  • After annexation the site is "legally nonconforming," "including exclusive application of federal and state standards regarding air quality and emissions." (Section 6.03(h))
  • The City's development code standards for "noise, smoke and particulate matter, odorous matter, fire or explosive hazard material, and toxic and noxious matter" apply "only as modified by the Development Standards." (Section 6.17)
  • Daimler notifies the City within 30 days of "any final environmental enforcement action" that "imposes a fine or requires material remediation," and of "serious construction-related incidents." (Section 3.06)

Other City commitments

  • No reinvestment zone, tax increment zone, transportation reinvestment zone, public improvement district or special assessment over the site without Daimler's consent. (Section 6.14)
  • A $50,000 infrastructure grant; a supplier attraction program; a project ombudsperson; letters of support for state incentives and road projects. (Exhibit C)
  • "will consider in good faith a substantially reduced cost of approximately nine (9) acres of City-owned property" for William Pettus Road right-of-way. (Exhibit C, item 8)
  • A traffic study is being prepared; it does "not create any obligation for the City or the Company ... to design, construct, fund, or contribute to the cost of any roadway or traffic improvement." (Section 6.20)

Other Daimler commitments

  • $50,000 a year to the City for local social services in 2027, 2028 and 2029. After that, organizations may apply to Daimler's "Daimler Truck Cares program." (Section 3.07)
  • An annual compliance certificate with job counts, wages and investment. (Section 9.01)

What rules apply on the site

Today, about 320 acres of the site are under the SMART Terminal agreement. The rest is unincorporated Caldwell County: "The County has no building code, fire code, zoning, or water quality regulations." (Commissioner Ed Theriot, 2023)11 Under the new agreements, the whole site would follow "Development Standards" in the interlocal agreement until annexation.

SMART Terminal agreement (today, about 320 acres)

  • Water quality: "at least 70 percent of increased total suspended solids are removed from stormwater and a water quality volume produced by a 1.25-inch rainfall is captured" (Section 2.01)
  • Impervious cover: "shall not exceed 70 percent of the entire gross acreage," 90 percent of any lot (Section 2.03)
  • Drainage: peak runoff "at least 10 percent less than existing pre-development peak rates for the 2, 10, 25 and 100-year storm events" (Section 2.04)
  • Noise under development code Section 7.4.2.1 (Section 2.05)
  • Smoke, odor and toxic emissions under development code Sections 7.4.2.2, 7.4.2.3 and 7.4.2.5; "manufacturing of finished products producing the release of volatile organic compounds, or toxic or poisonous emissions at levels unsafe for human inhalation will not be allowed" (Section 2.06)
  • Airport review and avigation easements (Section 2.07)
  • TCEQ and EPA spill and stormwater standards; "no variances from or waivers of TCEQ or EPA rules or regulations shall be permitted" (Section 2.08)
  • No storage of hazardous materials, "except fuels contained in vehicles"; no bulk fuel storage (Exhibit D)

  • "The review and approval of plats, permit applications, and related plans for the Project Site, and the inspection of construction on the Project Site, shall be conducted in accordance with the Development Standards and the Permitting Process." (Section 6.07)
  • The City's building, fire, plumbing, mechanical, electrical and energy codes apply before annexation: "the 2021 editions of the International Codes and the 2020 National Electrical Code." (Section 6.07)

Removing the land from the SMART Terminal agreement

The SMART Terminal agreement gives the property 2,108 "LUEs" (living unit equivalents) of City wastewater service, a reserved share of sewer capacity. Those LUEs came from the Cotton Center agreement.

  • SMART Terminal agreement, Section 1.07.D.3: "The City confirms that, as originally allocated in the Cotton Center Development Agreement, Owner shall be entitled to use of 2,108 LUEs of Wastewater Service on the Property."10 The Cotton Center agreement was terminated in November 2025 (Res. 2025-212R).6
  • SMART Terminal agreement, Section 1.05.A.3: "all water and wastewater impact fees to be charged by the City during the development of the Property will be the fees in effect as of March 19, 2019."10
  • The amendment removing the 319.589 acres, Section 2.3: "All other terms and conditions of the Development Agreement shall remain in force and effect, including ... the City's commitment of 2,108 LUEs of wastewater service for the Property as described on Exhibit A."3
  • The Daimler agreement, Section 5.02: "The reserved capacity shall be allocated to the Project Site from the 2,108 LUEs of wastewater service confirmed for the land covered by the City Development Agreement ... and the City Development Agreement Release shall include the release by the Developer ... of its claim to the allocated LUEs in favor of the Project Site."1
  • The amendment's Attachment 1 (new property description) and Attachment 2 (new land use plan) are placeholders reading "[Property Description]" and "[Conceptual Land Use Plan]."3 The City's concept plan for the remaining land says "THE TOTAL PROJECT AREA IS ±1,680 ACRES."12

Project Horizon Mill B No Submission — City Could Not Support Steel Manufacturing / Direct Reduced Iron (DRI)
RFI Date: January 2026
Source: Texas Governor's Office EDT
Site Need: 900 acres
Investment: Not disclosed
Jobs: Not disclosed
NAICS: 3311 (Iron and Steel Mills)

Operations

U.S. steel company. Integrated steel mill + DRI (direct reduced iron) facility. NAICS 3311. Requires 900 acres.

Water / Wastewater

9,000,000 GPD (9 MGD) water demand. 1,300,000 GPD (1.3 MGD) wastewater. City's total water usage is approximately 18 MGD — this project alone would require ~50% of city supply.

City Decision

Moved to No Submission. Water demand is physically impossible to meet without massive regional infrastructure investment.

City determined it could not support this project. 9 MGD water demand equals approximately half of the city's total water usage. No submission was made.

  • City correctly identified this as infeasible without major regional water supply expansion.

Project Star No Submission — City Could Not Support High-Tech Manufacturing (Solar / Semiconductor)
RFI Date: February 2026
Source: Texas Governor's Office EDT
Site Need: 700+ acres
Investment: $2 billion
Jobs: 675 Year 1 / 2,500 within 3–5 years
Decision Date: March 31, 2026
Target Production Start: March 1, 2027

Operations

High-tech manufacturing. Job types include metallization, PECVD, PVD (physical vapor deposition), and laser cutting — consistent with solar panel or semiconductor manufacturing.

Water / Wastewater

Large water demand (exact figure not specified in summary). City Utilities Director explicitly stated the city cannot support the requested water usage on a one-year timeline.

City Decision

Utilities Director: "We cannot support with that much water usage just a year from now. Have to pass."

City of San Marcos Response

City Utilities Director determined the city cannot support the project's water demands within the one-year timeline to production start. No submission made. Decision was driven by infrastructure timeline, not permanent capacity limitations.

  • $2B investment and 2,500 jobs would have been transformative for Hays County.
  • March 2027 production start was likely the fatal constraint — insufficient time to build out water infrastructure.
  • PECVD/PVD job types strongly suggest solar panel or semiconductor chip manufacturing.
Source: Governor's Office RFI documents, February 2026

Project North Star Active — Decision July/August 2026 Advanced Magnetics Manufacturing (U.S. company)
RFI Date: March 2026
Source: Texas Governor's Office EDT
Site Need: Not specified (10,000 MT annual capacity)
Investment: $1.8 billion
Jobs: 700 (197 within 2–3 years)
NAICS: 3346 (Magnetic & Optical Media Mfg.)
Decision Date: July / August 2026
Production Start: Q2 2029

Operations

U.S. magnetics company producing "world's first high-performance rare-earth-free permanent magnets." 10,000 metric tons annual capacity. Strategically important for defense and EV supply chain.

Water / Wastewater

1,400,000 GPD (1.4 MGD) water. 800,000 GPD (0.8 MGD) wastewater. City notes water is "high but manageable" and encourages water reuse. Wastewater deemed acceptable.

Critical Infrastructure Note

SH 80 Lift Station (wastewater) completion updated to Summer 2028 per city email — aligns with Q2 2029 production start. Previously estimated 2029; now accelerated.

City of San Marcos Response

City finds water demand "high but manageable" and encourages water reuse strategies. Wastewater is acceptable. SH 80 Lift Station CIP completion updated to Summer 2028, which supports the Q2 2029 production start timeline. Site visit expected April/May 2026.

  • Most strategically significant project in pipeline: rare-earth-free magnets are critical for EV motors, wind turbines, and defense applications — currently dominated by Chinese supply chain.
  • Q2 2029 production start provides more realistic infrastructure runway than most other projects.
  • $1.8B investment and 700 jobs — second largest by investment in the active pipeline.
  • Site visit expected April/May 2026; final decision July/August 2026.
Source: Governor's Office RFI / North Star emails, March 2026
Project Vista Prism Active — In Discussion Electronics Manufacturing (international company — possibly Sony, Panasonic, or Sharp)
RFI Date: March 2026
Source: Texas Governor's Office EDT
Site Need: 500+ acres greenfield; 2.9M sf per building (2 buildings)
Investment: Not disclosed
Jobs: Not disclosed
Power Demand: 153.1 MW electricity

Operations

International electronics company. Potentially Sony, Panasonic, or Sharp based on company profile. Two buildings of ~2.9M sf each — massive manufacturing campus.

Water / Wastewater

576,700 gallons/hour in Phases 2 & 3 (~13.8 MGD at peak). This approaches Project Horizon Mill B levels and represents a very large fraction of city water supply.

Power Demand

153.1 MW electricity demand — an extraordinary power requirement that would need direct coordination with ERCOT/utility provider. Larger than many small cities' entire power consumption.

City of San Marcos Response

Still in discussion as of March 2026. The massive water demand (potentially 13+ MGD at peak) and 153 MW power requirement would present extraordinary infrastructure challenges. No formal city determination documented yet.

  • If the 576,700 gal/hour figure is peak usage across phases, it equals ~13.8 MGD — roughly 75% of the city's total water supply capacity.
  • 153 MW power demand would require dedicated transmission infrastructure.
  • Two buildings of 2.9M sf each = ~5.8M total sf — by far the largest footprint in the pipeline.
  • Still in early discussion; no city feasibility determination yet.
Source: Governor's Office RFI documents, March 2026

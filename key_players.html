PEOPLE, BUSINESSES & CORPORATE STRUCTURE

Key Players

The individuals involved in the development, permitting, and facilitation of the TriTexas Logistics Park in San Marcos, Texas.

All information on this page is drawn from public records, city permitting documents, and government filings obtained through Public Information Requests. Source documents are available upon request.

Developer

Scarborough Lane Development is the firm of record for the TriTexas Logistics Park project. It is controlled by El Paso billionaire Paul L. Foster through Franklin Mountain San Marcos I, LP. The individuals below represent Scarborough Lane in city communications, permitting applications, and pre-development meetings with City of San Marcos staff.

James Feagin
Scarborough Lane Development / Landmark Interests
Founder-emeritus, Scarborough Lane; Founder & President, Landmark Interests

Founder of Scarborough Lane Development (the developer of record) and founder/president of its brokerage arm, Landmark Interests. He is not an officer of Franklin Mountain - the Foster entity that owns the land - but is listed as tax/property contact on Caldwell CAD records for all FM-I LP parcels, and his name appears in city communications and permitting records spanning the full permitting period. See Ownership Structure for the full corporate chain.

Ryan Burkhardt
Scarborough Lane Development / Landmark Interests
Principal

Burkhardt attended the May 2022 pre-development meeting with City of San Marcos staff as a developer representative and is regularly copied on permitting correspondence. He holds roles at both Scarborough Lane Development and a related entity, Landmark Interests, and is named as a "Prepared By" party on project documents.

Clayton Kendall
Scarborough Lane Development
Representative

The most frequently appearing developer representative in city records, with his name appearing in 167 documents. Kendall attended the 2022 pre-development meeting alongside Burkhardt and is regularly included on permitting and engineering correspondence between the developer team and City of San Marcos staff.

Scot Whitwer
Scarborough Lane Development
Representative

Whitwer is named in permitting coordination emails between the developer and city engineering staff, including correspondence about construction inspections and conditional permit releases for the Phase I public improvement construction plan.

Lead Applicant Engineer

Kimley-Horn, a national civil engineering firm, served as the primary engineering consultant for the developer throughout the permitting process.

Ben Ruby
Kimley-Horn
Lead Engineer, P.E. - San Antonio Office

Ruby served as the lead licensed engineer on all major permitting submissions for the project, including the Public Improvement Construction Plan (PICP), Watershed Protection Plan (WPP), Conditional Letter of Map Revision (CLOMR), and plat applications. His name and email appear more than 860 times across PIR documents - more than any other individual - reflecting his central role in the day-to-day engineering and permitting work.

San Marcos City Council

The Mayor and six Council Members are elected at-large to staggered terms and hold final authority over annexation ordinances, development agreement amendments, and zoning changes tied to the project. Below is every identified piece of City Council legislation touching the project (2019–2025) with each then-serving member's vote where it is individually recorded. The City has still not published finalized minutes for any meeting after roughly mid-2022. What it did release, in response to a public information request answered in August 2026, are the unapproved draft minutes for the 2022–2025 meetings - and those drafts do record individual votes. Every post-2022 vote below is therefore sourced to a draft, and is marked (draft minutes) to say so. Drafts are working documents that have not been adopted by Council, and the released set contains demonstrable transcription errors - most seriously, the September 3, 2024 draft scrambles Council members' first and last names throughout its roll call. Surname-only vote tallies are used here in preference to any full name appearing in those drafts. Where the record is still silent, that is marked rather than guessed at. 2026 is an election year: Mayor Hughson is not seeking re-election as Mayor and is instead running against incumbent Alyssa Garza for Place 3; Amanda Rodriguez has announced a run for Mayor.

Each card opens with a record strip - one square per contested project vote, left to right in date order, so you can see at a glance which way a member leaned and whether that changed over time. Hover any square for the item and the member's vote. Only contested votes are scored. A unanimous vote says nothing about which way someone leans, and several of the biggest decisions here were unanimous - including the 6–0 vote that expanded the project to roughly 2,017 acres. Counting those would flatten every member toward “supportive” regardless of how they behaved when the Council actually divided. Ten contested votes are scored, from January 2019 to September 2024. Votes on a motion to postpone or to deny are inverted, because voting for one of those was a vote against the project advancing. Where one question produced two votes at a single meeting - August 5, 2024, where a motion to deny failed and the approval then carried - it counts once. The strip is a summary of the itemised record below it, not a substitute for it, and it inherits the draft-minutes caveat above for everything after 2022.

The badge is a plain description of the count beside it, on a fixed scale: Consistent means every contested vote went one way; Mostly means at least two thirds did; Leans means a simple majority did, which on a five-vote record is a 3–2 margin and should be read as weak. No member currently sits at an even split. Members with short tenures have small denominators, so a badge is never stronger evidence than the number of squares behind it.

On the Ballot — November 3, 2026

Three council seats and the Mayor’s office are before voters. Where a candidate has a Council voting record on this project, the same record strip used below is shown for them. Where a candidate has none, the row says No record and shows an empty strip. An empty strip means no evidence, not a moderate position — it should never be read as a middle ground between the two coloured rows. Positions taken in campaign statements, endorsements and contributions are a different and weaker class of evidence and are deliberately not mixed in here. Candidate names, filings and withdrawals are from the City’s official list, retrieved Aug. 26, 2026 (sanmarcostx.gov/4117); a field can still change.

Key Players

The individuals involved in the development, permitting, and facilitation of the TriTexas Logistics Park in San Marcos, Texas.

Back to main page
All information on this page is drawn from public records, city permitting documents, and government filings obtained through Public Information Requests. Source documents are available upon request.

Developer

Scarborough Lane Development is the firm of record for the TriTexas Logistics Park project. It is controlled by El Paso billionaire Paul L. Foster through Franklin Mountain San Marcos I, LP. The individuals below represent Scarborough Lane in city communications, permitting applications, and pre-development meetings with City of San Marcos staff.

James Feagin
Axis Logistics Park / Franklin Mountain
Chairman & CEO

Named principal of the development entities associated with the TriTexas Logistics Park. Feagin appears in city communications related to the project and signed or was party to key development agreements. His name appears in records obtained through Public Information Requests spanning the full permitting period.

Ryan Burkhardt
Scarborough Lane Development / Landmark Interests
Principal

Burkhardt attended the May 2022 pre-development meeting with City of San Marcos staff as a developer representative and is regularly copied on permitting correspondence. He holds roles at both Scarborough Lane Development and a related entity, Landmark Interests, and is named as a "Prepared By" party on project documents.

Clayton Kendall
Scarborough Lane Development
Representative

The most frequently appearing developer representative in city records, with his name appearing in 167 documents. Kendall attended the 2022 pre-development meeting alongside Burkhardt and is regularly included on permitting and engineering correspondence between the developer team and City of San Marcos staff.

Scot Whitwer
Scarborough Lane Development
Representative

Whitwer is named in permitting coordination emails between the developer and city engineering staff, including correspondence about construction inspections and conditional permit releases for the Phase I public improvement construction plan.

Lead Applicant Engineer

Kimley-Horn, a national civil engineering firm, served as the primary engineering consultant for the developer throughout the permitting process.

Ben Ruby
Kimley-Horn
Lead Engineer, P.E. — San Antonio Office

Ruby served as the lead licensed engineer on all major permitting submissions for the project, including the Public Improvement Construction Plan (PICP), Watershed Protection Plan (WPP), Conditional Letter of Map Revision (CLOMR), and plat applications. His name and email appear more than 860 times across PIR documents — more than any other individual — reflecting his central role in the day-to-day engineering and permitting work.

City of San Marcos

Although the project site sits primarily in Caldwell County, the City of San Marcos has jurisdiction over the property through its Extraterritorial Jurisdiction (ETJ), utility service agreements, annexation proceedings, and a 2023 Development Agreement. The following city officials reviewed, approved, and coordinated on those matters.

Jane Hughson
City of San Marcos
Mayor

Mayor Hughson was kept informed of annexation proceedings related to the project and raised questions through the city's Council Forum about the alignment of a planned road through the development — specifically whether it was positioned to run behind existing residential properties.

Joe Pantalion
City of San Marcos — City Manager's Office
Assistant City Manager, P.E.

Pantalion attended the May 2022 I-Zone pre-development meeting and served as a primary city leadership contact for the project. He coordinated meetings between city staff and the developer to discuss wastewater solutions — a documented constraint on the development — and responded to public concerns about the AN-24-03 road annexation.

Richard Reynosa
City of San Marcos — Engineering
Assistant Director of Engineering, P.E./CFM

Reynosa was the primary city engineering contact for all permitting on the project, reviewing and responding to PICP, WPP, and CLOMR submissions. He appears in more than 28 PIR documents and coordinated the city's response to drainage, roadway, and utility questions raised by the developer's engineering team.

Amanda Hernandez
City of San Marcos — Planning
Planner, AICP/CNU-A/CPM

Hernandez was the lead planning contact for the project, attending the 2022 pre-development meeting and handling planning review for annexations, zoning, and the development agreement. She served as a point of contact between the developer, engineering staff, and the City Manager's Office on planning matters related to the project.

Tyler Hjorth
City of San Marcos — Utilities
Director of Utilities

Hjorth attended the 2022 pre-development meeting as the city's utilities authority and was a key contact for discussions about water, wastewater, and electric service to the development. He was later included in correspondence about a proposed regional sewer infrastructure plan along Highway 80 East, which would affect the project's ability to obtain wastewater service.

Greg Schwarz
City of San Marcos — Engineering
Senior Engineer

Schwarz served as the City's primary technical reviewer for the project's watershed protection plan (WPP) submissions. He presented the Axis Logistics Park QWPP Phase 2 (Permit #2024-49696) to the P&Z Commission on August 27, 2024, and issued the R1 comment letter for the Edwards Aquifer parcel (Permit #2025-55457) flagging impervious cover violations, missing TCEQ permits, and peak flow increases for all regulated storm events. He also addressed floodplain and drainage regulations at the January 8, 2019 P&Z reconsideration hearing.

Shannon Mattingly
City of San Marcos — Planning
Director of Development Services (2018–2019)

Mattingly was the senior planning official during the initial annexation and zoning proceedings in 2018–2019. She staffed the January 15, 2019 City Council public hearing on Ord. 2019-01 and 2019-02, and acknowledged on the public record at the December 11, 2018 P&Z meeting that the ETJ mapping discrepancy between San Marcos and Martindale required review.

Tory Carpenter
City of San Marcos — Planning
Planner (2018–2019)

Carpenter presented the ZC-18-21 zoning case to the Planning & Zoning Commission at both the December 11, 2018 hearing (which ended in denial) and the January 8, 2019 reconsideration hearing (which resulted in approval 7–1).

Julia Cleary
City of San Marcos — Planning
Senior Planner

Cleary served as the lead planning contact for the 2023 re-application phase, presenting ZC-23-01 to the P&Z Commission on March 28, 2023 with a staff recommendation of approval. She was also involved in annexation coordination for AN-24-03 (the Energy Parkway ROW annexation).

Economic Development

The Hays-Caldwell Economic Development Partnership (EDP) is a public-private organization that markets sites in Hays and Caldwell counties to prospective industrial tenants. Internal city records show EDP staff routed confidential Requests for Information (RFIs) for large industrial projects directly to city engineering staff, specifically proposing Axis Logistics Park as a candidate site.

Emily Mathes
Hays-Caldwell Economic Development Partnership
Vice President of Economic Development

Mathes sent city engineering staff a confidential RFI for "Project Vortex Wind" — a proposed battery cell and pack assembly facility with a capital investment of up to $2.3 billion and 2,000 jobs — explicitly recommending Axis Logistics Park as the preferred site and asking city staff to respond to utility capacity questions on a tight deadline. The RFI originated from the Office of the Governor.

Sarah Martin
Hays-Caldwell Economic Development Partnership
Project Manager

Martin forwarded a separate RFI for "Project Frontier" — an architectural products manufacturer seeking 60-80 acres with a $178 million capital investment — to city engineering staff and asked them to confirm water and wastewater capacity for the Axis Logistics Park site. The RFI was distributed through Opportunity Austin, a regional economic development organization.

State of Texas

The Office of the Governor's Economic Development and Tourism division actively recruits large industrial projects to Texas and connects them with local sites and incentives. Records show the office was involved in directing at least one major prospective tenant toward the Axis Logistics Park site.

Lauren Slusher
Office of the Governor — Economic Development & Tourism
Industry Recruitment Specialist

Slusher is an industry recruitment specialist with the Governor's Economic Development and Tourism office, which received the initial inquiry for "Project Vortex Wind" from a consulting firm representing a prospective battery manufacturing tenant. The office then passed the RFI to the Hays-Caldwell EDP for regional response, with Axis Logistics Park identified as the candidate site.

Caldwell County

The project site sits in Caldwell County. The following county official appeared on the public record in connection with the project.

Ed Theriot
Caldwell County
County Commissioner, Precinct 3

Theriot appeared at the January 8, 2019 P&Z Commission meeting and made himself available to answer questions about the county's role in the project. He referenced a county 381 Agreement, EDA Grants, and roadway projects planned by TxDOT and the Capital Area Metropolitan Planning Organization (CAMPO) related to Loop 110, US-80, and State Highway 21 — signaling county-level coordination and support for the SMART Terminal project at its inception. The project site falls within Precinct 3.

Contractors

The following companies and individuals appear in city permitting and construction coordination records as contractors performing physical work on the project site and associated infrastructure.

HR Excavation
HR Excavation — Royse City, TX
Site Contractor — Energy Parkway

HR Excavation performed clearing and grading work on the Energy Parkway road corridor, a key piece of Phase I infrastructure. Construction coordination emails show the company notifying City of San Marcos engineering inspectors of planned work dates and sending site photos for inspection. Carlos Lopez served as field crew supervisor; Stephen Wheat also appears in construction correspondence.

Patin Construction LLC
Patin Construction LLC — Taylor, TX
Site Contractor

Patin Construction, a family-owned general contractor based in Taylor, Texas specializing in site grading, earthwork, and utilities across Central Texas, appears in project records. Oscar Tovar is the company representative named in correspondence related to the project.

Yarrington Road Materials
Yarrington Road Materials — San Marcos, TX
Materials Supplier — Concrete & Aggregates

Yarrington Road Materials, a Central Texas ready-mix concrete and aggregates producer serving the Austin and San Antonio markets, appears in project infrastructure correspondence. John Petersen, Manager of Operations, is the named representative. The company was copied on communications about the proposed Highway 80 East regional sewer infrastructure planning.
